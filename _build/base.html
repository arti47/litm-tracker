🎲 How to play

The game in one line

  • Describe what your Hero does → if it's risky, roll 2d6 + Power → read the outcome → spend Power on Effects → take any Consequences → repeat.

Building Power (before you roll)

  • Tags — tap each tag that helps (+1) or hinders (−1): your theme power tags, backpack, scene tags, the Fellowship.
  • Burn a tag 🔥 for +3 instead of +1 (it gets scratched).
  • Status — your single highest helpful and single highest hindering status count.
  • Might — set Favored (+3/+6) or Imperiled (−3/−6) when the task is below/above your theme's Might.

Reading the dice

  • 10+ — success, no consequences.
  • 7–9 — success, but with a consequence/cost.
  • 6 or less — it goes wrong; take the consequences.

Growing your Hero

  • Invoke a weakness → mark Improve. Achieve a Quest → Milestone. Betray one → Abandon. Fill a track (3) to develop the theme.
  • Evolving/replacing a theme marks Promise; fill 5 Promise → a Moment of Fulfillment (gain a Quintessence).
  • Camp (☰ menu) between scenes to recover scratched tags and reduce statuses.

Playing without a Narrator? Turn on ☰ → Solo Play Mode for the 🔮 Oracle, then see the Oracle tab's help.

Might · Favored & Imperiled

Themes come in three Mights: Origin (ordinary) → Adventure (heroic) → Greatness (legendary). When you act, compare the Might the task demands to the Might you bring (your relevant theme/tag).

Matchedtask is about your level — no Might modifier (0).
Favored +3the task is below your Might — you outclass it.
Extr. Favored +6the task is far below your Might.
Imperiled −3the task is above your Might — out of your depth.
Extr. Imperiled −6the task is far above your Might.
Whereset this with the Might control on the Roll tab; the Narrator calls it.

10+Success — spend your Power on Effects. No Consequences.
7–9Success & Consequences — spend Power, but take a hit too.
6−Consequences — no Success.
⚅⚅Double sixes = Success without Consequences, any Power.
⚀⚀Double ones = Consequences without Success, any Power.
Min 1On a Success you always have at least 1 Power to spend.

Camping

Expire story tags → establish the place → each Hero takes 2 activities (3 with Consequences): Rest (recover statuses & scratched power tags), Reflect (mark Improve on a theme), Camp Action (count Power, spend half without rolling, or roll). 🔮 Solo Play — how to use the Oracle

In solo (or co-op) play, the Oracle replaces the Narrator. You drive the fiction; ask the Oracle whenever you'd need a Narrator answer. Enable it via ☰ → Enable Solo Play Mode — the 🔮 Oracle tab then appears.

The solo loop
1 · FrameSet the Scene card (Tracking tab): stakes, threats, game loop step. Roll Conflict / Scene on the Oracle tab if you don't know what's here yet — it gives you 7 columns (Central Challenge, Role, Target, Location…). Add interesting results as Scene story tags.
2 · AskWhenever you want a Narrator answer, use Question — Yes/No: tap the ± Power stepper up/down for tags favouring or opposing the outcome, then roll. Double 1 = Extreme No (and worse); Double 6 = Extreme Yes (and more). For a flavourful prompt use Question — Interpretive (d66 → a symbol + attitude/terrain/being/item sub-columns).
3 · ActTake your action on the Roll tab as normal — tap your tags, set Might, roll 2d6 + Power. 10+ clean success; 7–9 success with a consequence; 6− consequences only.
4 · SpendOn a success, the Effects panel lets you inflict foe statuses, add backpack tags, advance Progress, recover tags. Use 👹 Add foe status (Tracking tab) to track what you put on enemies.
5 · ConsequenceOn a 7–9 or 6−, roll Consequences (d66) on the Oracle tab to find out what the world throws back. Or roll Challenge Action — pick the foe's Role, roll d6 for its Threat. Then react on the Roll tab (switch to Reaction mode).
6 · Log itEvery Oracle roll auto-saves to the Oracle log (bottom of the Oracle tab). Add a note on each entry: the question you asked, the result, and the fiction you decided it means. End each entry with a loose thread — the next question you'll bring to the Oracle.
7 · CampBetween scenes: ☰ → Camp / Sojourn to recover scratched tags, reduce statuses, and grow your themes.

Good journaling
What to writeEach Oracle log note: (1) the question / action, (2) the dice result, (3) the fiction you derived from it, (4) a loose end — the next question. This last part is what keeps the story moving.
JourneyFor travel between locations use ☰ → Journey montage: set destination + legs-to-arrival track, roll Vignette Challenges along the way (🎲 jumps you to the Roll tab; ✓ advances a leg).
Big revealsUse Revelations at major turning points — Act I sets up, Act II twists, Act III resolves. Let the result reframe everything you thought you knew.

🎯 How to run a game

The whole arc in one place — from a blank screen to a satisfying ending. Do these in order; you'll be playing within minutes.

▶ Starting — your first 10 minutes
  1. Get a hero fast. On the Welcome screen tap 📦 Ready-made Hero (build your own later). You now have tags and dice.
  2. Playing alone? Tick "I'm playing solo" (or ☰ → Solo Play). The 🔮 Oracle appears — it plays the GM for you.
  3. Frame one scene. Tracking → 🎬 Scene. Write one goal in Stakes (e.g. "find what's killing the sheep") and where you are. That's enough to start.
  4. (Solo) See what's here. Oracle → Conflict / Scene → roll, and turn the result into a sentence of fiction. Now something is happening.
🔁 Sustaining — one loop, repeated (this is the game)
  1. Say what your hero does.
  2. Risky or uncertain? → Roll tab: tap the tags that help, then Roll. 10+ clean · 7–9 it works but something bites · 6− it goes wrong.
  3. Spend your Power in the panel that appears — that's how you change the world (give a foe a status, grab a tag, fill a Progress track).
  4. (Solo) on a 7–9 / 6−, tap 🔮 Roll a Consequence to see what bites back, then 📓 Log to journal.
  5. Ask Oracle → Yes/No whenever you'd ask a GM "is that true?"
  6. When the scene's goal is settled (won or lost), start a new scene — new Stakes; use 🧭 Journey if you travel.
  7. Camp between hard scenes to heal and grow.
The one trick that keeps momentum: end every journal note with a new question. "The beast fled — but whose footprints are those?" That question is your next scene.
⏹️ Ending well — three sizes of ending
  • End a scene: when the Stakes are resolved, narrate the result and set the 🎬 loop to ③ Consequences. Note what changed, then move on.
  • End a session: open 🏕️ Camp — expire used-up tags, Rest (recover), Reflect (mark Improve to grow). Then write the loose threads in your journal. That's your save point.
  • End a story: each theme carries a Quest. Pursue it → mark Milestone; enough growth fills Promise to 5 → a Moment of Fulfillment (a permanent reward and a natural turning point). A story ends well when the central Quest resolves — fulfilled or abandoned. Narrate a short denouement, mark that last Milestone, and note how the hero changed. Credits roll.
